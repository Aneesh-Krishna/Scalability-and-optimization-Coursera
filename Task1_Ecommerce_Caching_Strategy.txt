TASK 1: IDENTIFY WHAT TO CACHE - E-COMMERCE STORE CACHING STRATEGY
====================================================================

Scenario: An online store selling electronics, clothing, and home goods is
suffering from slow page loads, high server load when fetching product
details, and slow shopping cart retrieval. The strategy below uses an
in-memory cache (e.g., Redis or Memcached) in front of the database.

WHY THESE EXPIRATION TIMES?
---------------------------

- Product details change rarely, so a 30-minute TTL plus invalidation on
  edit gives high cache hit rates while still showing updates right away.

- Prices and stock change often and directly affect money and orders, so
  they get the shortest TTL, are invalidated on change, and are always
  re-checked at checkout. A slightly stale page is acceptable, but a wrong
  charge or an oversold item is not.

- Shopping carts belong to one user and change through that user's actions,
  so they are updated on every change instead of waiting for a timer. A
  1-hour inactivity expiry frees memory from abandoned carts, and writing
  through to the database means no cart is lost.

- Search results are expensive to compute, and popular searches repeat
  constantly. A 10-15 minute TTL keeps them reasonably fresh while removing
  most of the search load.

- Categories, menus, and homepage content change on a schedule set by the
  business, so longer TTLs or invalidating when the business updates them
  work well.

- Sessions use a sliding inactivity timeout for security, so an idle
  session expires on its own.


DATA THAT SHOULD NOT BE CACHED (OR ONLY BRIEFLY)
------------------------------------------------

- Payment details and final order totals at checkout: always read from the
  database so they are correct and secure.
- Final stock check when placing an order: must use the database to prevent
  overselling.
